Exercise 1.

In this lab, we reproduce the calculation carried out in Press and Teukolsky (1988). The calculation will be directly applicable to Problem 2 of Homework #4. We will be applying a Kolmogorov-Smirnov-type analysis to a 2D RV distribution, although the CDF for a 2D distribution is not well-defined.

  1. Create a Jupyter Notebook press.ipynb for this calculation.

  2. Start by reading in the 2D data contained in Press and Teukolsky Figure 1, from the files ptdata.squares.dat and ptdata.triangles.dat. (These data were taken directly from Figure 1 by the instructor.) It would be a good idea to make a scatter plot of both datasets, to compare with Press and Teukolsky's figure.



  3. Create a function quadct to return the normalized fraction in each quadrant. This will be nothing more than a Python version of Press and Teukolsky's SUBROUTINE QUADCT (originally in FORTRAN).

  4. Create a function QKS to compute KS probability (Equation 3 of Press and Teukolsky). You obviously can't count an infinite number of terms, so you'll need to pick a cutoff for j. Start by choosing jmax = 10, later test if your results change when you vary this.

  5. Create a function ks2d2s which calls quadct and QKS, to generate the 2D KS statistic and chance probability.

  6. Lastly, your main script should call ks2d2s, with the x- and y-values of the two RVs (four arrays of numbers in all).

  7. As a last step, print out your results in the format 
           The 2D KS statistic is: x.xxxxx 
       The probability is:     x.xxxxx
    and compare your results with those of Press and Teukolsky. If your agreement is reasonable, you may proceed to Homework #4 with confidence.
