Exercise 1.

A physics instructor, disappointed at his end-of-semester ratings, decides that what is missing from his class is a little humor. He resolves to start telling more jokes in lecture, and begins to carefully tabulate the number of jokes he tells and his ratings. The results are as follows:

Year Number of Jokes Rating
1 12 5.1
2 18 4.1
3 33 3.8
4 27 3.3
5 39 3.6

Following the methods described in lecture, prepare a Jupyter Notebook named jokes.ipynb which calculates and outputs the following:

  • A plot of Rating versus the Number of Jokes told.

  • The quantities σx, σy, and σxy. (In this case, the variables x and y refer to “number of jokes” and “rating” respectively.)

  • Pearson's correlation coefficient r.

  • The chance probability or p-value of obtaining this value of r with five data points.

  • Your notebook should conclude with a statement of any advice you have for the physics instructor.

If time allows, also check your calculations above using numpy and scipy tools mentioned in lecture.
